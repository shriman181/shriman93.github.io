The Problem

Infrastructure is aging.
Inspection is broken.

The world's critical infrastructure — bridges, pipelines, dams, transmission towers — was built for a 50-year lifespan. Most of it is well past that window.

City governments face a paradox: manual inspection at scale is prohibitively expensive, requires shutdowns, and exposes workers to dangerous environments. Yet scheduled inspections happen so infrequently that failures occur between cycles.

The American Society of Civil Engineers grades US infrastructure a C−. Over 7,500 bridges are structurally deficient. Pipeline incidents cause billions in economic damage annually. Aging power grid failures are accelerating.

The status quo is not sustainable. A new inspection paradigm isn't a luxury — it's an emergency.

P-001
Manual Inspection at Scale is Infeasible
Rope access teams and underwater divers cost $8–40K per structure per inspection cycle. City budgets support annual inspections at best — quarterly is impossible.
P-002
Scheduled Inspections Miss Rapid Degradation
Seismic events, thermal cycling, and corrosion-accelerating environments cause structural changes in weeks. Yearly inspections leave blind windows that kill people.
P-003
Human Inspectors Have Cognitive Limits
Micro-cracks under 0.1mm, internal corrosion, and subsurface deformation are invisible to the human eye. By the time damage is visible, structural integrity is already compromised.
P-004
Zero Centralized Intelligence
Inspection reports sit in PDFs and spreadsheets. No city has a real-time structural health dashboard. No deterioration trend exists. Decisions are made on incomplete data.
